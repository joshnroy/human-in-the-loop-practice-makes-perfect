**TL;DR** Ball-Ring's `PlaceBallOnTable` records 0/3280 practice successes because it is **impossible by design** (`place_ball_fall_prob = 1.0`), not because anything is broken — proven, not assumed. The interesting findings are what EES does about it: all **3280/3280** executions land in the `UNINFORMATIVE` sampler pool with **0/3280** epsilon-random despite `--exploration-epsilon 0.5`; **3275/3280** executions are because EES *chose* it as the practice target; and its share of all practice targets **rises** from 473/1802 to 1294/2054, so EES never learns to stop. Separately, the published 99/100 arm and PR #126's 91/100 are **provably different computations** (they diverge at the first practice checkpoint in 10/10 seeds) but the better-powered whole-curve test does **not** support either being worse (p = 0.109). No EES or Ball-Ring behaviour is changed.

## Question / goal

PR #126's audit found `PlaceBallOnTable` at 0 successes in 3280 practice executions, 0 in every one of 10 seeds, while EES selected it as a practice target in 10/10 seeds — and explicitly left it uninvestigated. Distinguish four explanations that need four different fixes: (a) its add-effects are unsatisfiable as written; (b) its controller genuinely cannot achieve them; (c) its success check disagrees with the dynamics (Tossing3D's `NearBin` defect, inverted); (d) it is only ever attempted from states where it cannot succeed. Then answer what a "by design" verdict does not cover: which sampler pool the executions land in, whether they are targets or plan-prefix side-effects, and whether EES ever stops targeting it.

Secondarily: explain, or establish as not determinable, why the published `iters10k` arm scored 99/100 where #126's re-run at identical settings and the same fixed seeds scored 91/100.

## Background

`environments/ballring/` ports predicators' `ball_and_cup_sticky_table`. Ten of its fifteen lifted skills are `param_dim = 0`; the five placements carry `param_dim = 2` and are what a learning `Method` must tune. Three records make this answerable without new runs: PR #111 gave `stats.json` a per-skill record of practice *executions*; PR #119 split its fallback pool so "no sampler exists" reads apart from "a sampler was consulted and could not discriminate"; PR #126 added `PracticeTargetTally`, a record of practice *decisions*, because an execution is not a decision — EES executes a chosen candidate's whole plan prefix on the way to it, which is exactly how Tossing3D's `MoveToThrowPose` recorded 175/175 executions while being dropped from every candidate list.

`BallRingSkills`' own docstring already asserts `PlaceBallOnTable` is deliberately impossible, the Ball-Ring analogue of Light Switch's `JumpToLight`. That assertion had never been checked against the dynamics, against the resolved config of any real run, or against EES's behaviour.

## Hypothesis

`PlaceBallOnTable` is impossible by construction (explanation **b**), and the substantive content is in EES's response to it rather than in the skill itself.

## Guidance given

Do not assume it is a defect — a skill that legitimately never succeeds in the states EES reaches is a domain-design fact. Verify or refute the docstring's claim rather than repeat it. Separate target selection from execution count; name the pool; say whether EES ever stops, and if it never does, say so plainly as a finding about the competence model rather than a licence to change EES. Do not change EES's behaviour. Never edit, restate or recompute a published number — add a staleness note beside it, in the committed log as well as here. Counts as `x/y`, never a bare percentage. Every quantitative result needs a figure with per-seed spread. Q1 is the priority and Q2 is the cut; do not launch a bisect grid for Q2.

## Methods

**No new simulation runs.** The 10 Ball-Ring runs are PR #126's, copied verbatim (`SHA256SUMS` records their digests) into this PR's own log directory so the analysis is self-contained and re-runnable from `main` rather than depending on an unmerged branch.

Three instruments:

- **A direct dynamics probe** (deliberately not committed — it drives the `Environment` directly, which an `analysis/` script must never do): `PlaceBallOnTable` executed from states where its preconditions genuinely hold, 20 sampled initial states × 5 tables × 50 parameter values, at the shipped config and at a `place_ball_fall_prob = 0.0` counterfactual.
- **`analysis/practice_makes_perfect/ballring_impossible_skill.py`** — pools, practice-target decisions, and selection share over training.
- **`analysis/practice_makes_perfect/ballring_published_vs_rerun.py`** — per-seed, per-checkpoint comparison of the published arm against #126's, with exact paired permutation tests on both the endpoint and the whole curve.

For Q2, a `git log` scan restricted to the paths Ball-Ring reads (`environments/ballring/`, `methods/practice_makes_perfect/`, `planning/`, `core/`, `practice_loop.py`, `cli.py`, `scripts/run_sweep.py`), plus `git log -S` over every Ball-Ring config field. No bisect sweep was run — see Recommendation 3.

## Results

### 1. Impossible by design — explanation (b), and (a), (c), (d) are refuted

In `BallRingEnvironment._handle_placing`, a place onto a table sets `fall_prob = place_ball_fall_prob` whenever the held object is the ball; the sticky-table branch below can only raise it to `1.0`, never lower it. At `place_ball_fall_prob = 1.0`, `uniform() < 1.0` is true for every draw, so the ball always falls. `place_ball_fall_prob = '1.0'` is confirmed in the **resolved** `args` of all 10 committed `config_snapshot.json`.

| `place_ball_fall_prob` | executions | `BallOnTable` held | `HandEmpty` held | both (= success) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1.0** (shipped) | 5000 | **0/5000** | 5000/5000 | **0/5000** |
| 0.0 (counterfactual) | 5000 | 4567/5000 | 5000/5000 | **4567/5000** |

**(a) refuted** — the identical add-effect conjunction is satisfiable 4567/5000 under a different config, and `BallOnTable` is achieved in the real runs by `PlaceBallInCupOnTable` (242/242). **(c) refuted** — `HandEmpty` holds 5000/5000 in both arms and `BallOnTable` tracks the ball's actual position; the success check *agrees* with the dynamics, the opposite of `NearBin`. **(d) refuted** — the impossibility is state-independent. **This is a domain-design fact, not a bug, and nothing here proposes changing it.**

### 2. All 3280 executions are `UNINFORMATIVE`; 0/3280 are epsilon-random

| pool | `PlaceBallOnTable` | `PlaceCupWithoutBallOnTable` (the decisive learnable skill) |
|---|---|---|
| `EPSILON_RANDOM` | **0/3280** | 1041/3190 |
| `INFORMED` | 0/3280 | 1067/3190 |
| `UNINFORMATIVE` | **3280/3280** | 1082/3190 |
| `NO_SAMPLER` | 0/3280 | 0/3190 |
| succeeded | **0/3280** | 2002/3190 |

`UNINFORMATIVE` had to be **derived** — `SkillPracticeTally` stores only three of the four pools, computing the fourth as `attempts − random − informed − unparameterized`; dropping the last term silently folds `NO_SAMPLER` in, so that arithmetic lives in one place and is pinned by a test.

**The 0/3280 epsilon-random is the surprising number.** With `--exploration-epsilon 0.5` the naive expectation is that ~half the draws are coin flips. It is zero for a structural reason in `LearnedSkillSampler.sample`: an all-negative label set triggers the single-class shortcut, every candidate scores identically, `best` spans all 100 candidates, and the `uninformative_tie_fraction` test returns a uniform draw **before** the epsilon-greedy branch is reached. An impossible skill is a single-class classifier problem, and this sampler answers it by never consulting epsilon at all.

### 3. Selection, not prefix

`num_selected = 3275` against 3280 executions, with `num_declined_perfect = 0` and `num_unreachable = 0`. At most 5/3280 executions could be prefix side-effects. Stated precisely: selections and executions are different events counted by different records, so this is an inference from their near-equality plus `num_unreachable = 0`, not a direct measurement. Making it direct needs one field — `was_target: bool` on `EesMethod._SkillAttempt` (which already carries `consultation`), tallied as `num_target_attempts`. A counter, not a behaviour change.

### 4. EES never learns to stop

| third of training (25 windows, thirds of 8) | `PlaceBallOnTable` selections / all practice-target selections |
|---|---|
| first | 473/1802 |
| middle | 1311/1996 |
| last | **1294/2054** |

Pooled, **3275/6078** of every practice target EES chose. It rises from roughly a quarter to roughly two thirds and then **plateaus** — "rises and stays high", not "grows without bound". Never once declined (`num_declined_perfect = 0/10` seeds).

The mechanism is a missing case in `EesMethod.score_ground_skill`: `skip_perfect` drops a candidate whose measured success rate is exactly **1.0**, and there is no symmetric rule for exactly **0.0**. Scoring substitutes the *optimistically extrapolated* competence for the candidate being scored, so an impossible skill — whose true competence sits at the `1e-12` floor and whose cost is therefore the largest of any step — promises the largest available plan-cost reduction. It is maximally attractive forever, and more attractive the longer it fails. The UCB bonus decays in `num_tries` and is outweighed.

Not obviously harmful here: runs still reach 91/100, because plan *cost* correctly routes the real task through `PlaceBallInCupOnTable` once competence collapses. What is wasted is practice budget — 3275/6078 of it.

### 5. The published 99/100 versus #126's 91/100

**Certain: the two arms are different computations.** A `--seed` fully determines a run, so identical code at the same seed must give an identical curve. They diverge at the **first** post-practice checkpoint (100 transitions) in 7/10 seeds and by the second (200 transitions) in the remaining 3/10.

**Not established: that either is worse.** The endpoint p = 0.0625 is exactly its own floor (5/10 pairs tied, 2 × 2⁻⁵) and so describes the design, not the world. Summing solved tasks over all 26 checkpoints gives an untied, better-powered paired statistic (floor 2 × 2⁻¹⁰ = 0.00195) that genuinely could have resolved a consistent shift. It does not: lower in **6/10** seeds, 1947/2600 against 2037/2600, exact paired permutation **p = 0.109**. The mean curves cross — the re-run learns faster to ~700 transitions and finishes lower.

**Provenance.** The published arm predates `config_snapshot.json` (PR #66, 2026-08-05) and its raw directories did not survive the machine move, so no provenance artifact exists; its absence is evidence of nothing. From `git log` its tree is equivalent to **`9f62b58`** for every Ball-Ring-relevant path — **inferred, not verified**. The re-run's own snapshots record `git_commit: 77ba55e` with **`git_dirty: true`**, i.e. pre-squash #119 plus an unrecorded local diff, and *not* `ebf3d92`.

**Excluded:** PR #112 / `OMP_NUM_THREADS` (already withdrawn by #126; `run_sweep.py` pinned it at `9f62b58`, so it is a no-op for both swept arms); PR #119 (its `wrapped_sampler.py` delta is one pure property, its `ees_method.py` delta stops gating a record on `explore`; no draw added or removed); PR #123 (touches only `environments/tossing3d/`, and postdates the re-run's tree). **No Ball-Ring config default changed** — `git diff 9f62b58 ebf3d92 -- src/hitl_pmp/environments/ballring/` is a single hunk (`noop_action()`).

**Leading candidate, not established: PR #85 (`3eb32c5`)**, "Fall back to a uniform draw when the sampler cannot discriminate", which sits between the two trees and changes both the number of `Generator` draws in `sample` and the returned candidate's distribution — on the very branch section 2 shows firing on 3280/3280 and 1082/3190 executions.

### Do Q1 and Q2 share a cause?

**They share a code path, not a cause.** Q1's impossibility is a domain-config fact (`place_ball_fall_prob = 1.0`) that predates PR #85 entirely and was equally true in the published arm. What #85 changed is how the sampler *responds* to that impossibility — the `UNINFORMATIVE` branch is exactly where all 3280 executions land, and #85 altered its draw count and output distribution. So Q1 explains why that branch is hot on Ball-Ring, which is why a change to it is a credible Q2 candidate; but fixing or not fixing Q1 would not have prevented Q2, and vice versa.

## Recommendation

1. **Change nothing in EES or Ball-Ring on the strength of this.** Sections 1–4 are measurement of a deliberate design fact and of EES behaving as written.
2. **Decide whether "practises a provably impossible skill 3275/6078 of the time" is acceptable.** It is the intended stress test, but `skip_perfect` has no floor to match its ceiling. The smallest honest version of a floor is to drop a candidate whose measured success rate is exactly 0.0 over some minimum attempt count. **That changes EES's behaviour and deviates from predicators, so it is Josh's call, not this PR's**, and it would need its own before/after experiment: it frees ~half the practice budget and could move Ball-Ring's score either way.
3. **To settle section 5, run three arms** at `9f62b58`, `3eb32c5~1` and `3eb32c5` (exact commands in the log entry). Arm 1 asks whether 99/100 reproduces at all; the pair isolates #85 as a paired 10-seed test and is the only thing that can *establish* rather than nominate a cause. Even that cannot fully settle it — the re-run's tree was `git_dirty: true`, leaving an unquantified term, and if arm 1 does not reproduce 99/100 the published provenance is simply lost.
4. **Add `num_target_attempts`** if the target-versus-prefix distinction is ever load-bearing again.
